# Decision Log — Development Acceleration Measurement

This decision log is the Explainability deliverable for the Development Acceleration Measurement of the `blitzy-cal` repository, recorded in fulfillment of the user's verbatim rule that "every non-trivial implementation decision MUST be documented with rationale" and that "the decision log is the single source of truth for 'why' decisions." Each row below captures a choice that a competent engineer could reasonably have made differently; rationale, risks, and reversibility are documented so any future maintainer can audit, contest, or reverse the choice without reading the implementation code.

The companion artifacts under `/blitzy/reports/acceleration/` (the analytical report `acceleration-report.md`, the executive deck `executive-presentation.html`, the observability template `dashboard.md`, and the onboarding `README.md`) reference this log by decision topic in addition to row number. Row numbers are stable for the nineteen mandatory rows (the original seventeen enumerated in the Agent Action Plan plus Rows 18 and 19 appended in response to QA Checkpoint 1) and may shift if future re-runs of the harness surface additional non-trivial decisions appended after Row 19. Where row numbers in companion artifacts differ from the numbers below, the topic name is the authoritative cross-reference.

> **How to read this log**
>
> Each row of the `## Decisions` table has seven columns:
>
> - **#** — Stable row number. The first seventeen rows are the mandatory decisions enumerated in the Agent Action Plan; Rows 18 and 19 are mandatory additions appended in response to QA Checkpoint 1 findings.
> - **Decision** — Short topic name for the choice being recorded.
> - **Alternatives Considered** — Two or more competing options that were evaluated, written as a brief enumeration so a reader can re-evaluate the choice without reconstructing the decision context.
> - **Choice** — The option that was selected, including any parameters or fallback behavior associated with the selection.
> - **Rationale** — Two to four sentences explaining why the choice was made. The Explainability rule forbids embedding this content in code comments; the cell here is the source of truth.
> - **Risks** — What could go wrong if the choice is incorrect, paired explicitly with the mitigation in place. A risk without a mitigation is not acceptable in this table; every Risks cell names both.
> - **Reversibility** — One of "High", "Medium", or "Low", followed by a one-sentence justification. "High" means a command-line flag or configuration change re-runs the analysis with the alternative; "Medium" means a localized code edit in a single function is required; "Low" means the choice is definitional and reversing it would invalidate the methodology.

### Suggested Reading Order

The log is structured so that different readers can enter at the section most relevant to their goal. The reading-order suggestions below cover the most common reader profiles.

- **Implementation reviewers** should read the Decisions table first, then the Implementation Anchors subsection to find each decision's code location, then the Risk Mitigation Index to verify each mitigation is in place. Total reading time: approximately fifteen minutes for a full review pass.
- **Methodology reviewers** should read the Decisions table first, then the Methodological Notes subsection to understand the cross-row relationships, then the Decision Provenance subsection to verify each choice has a documented origin. Total reading time: approximately ten minutes.
- **Compliance reviewers** should read the Deviations from Literal Interpretation section first to understand where literal rule satisfaction was not possible, then the Rule Grounding Matrix to verify each user rule maps to at least one decision or procedural artifact, then the Decision Validation Procedures to confirm machine-checkable rules pass. Total reading time: approximately twelve minutes.
- **Future maintainers** preparing to re-run the harness should read the Runtime Configuration Summary first, then the Per-Decision Reversibility Detail subsection to understand what each flag or constant controls, then the Future Watchpoints subsection to anticipate where new decisions may arise. Total reading time: approximately fifteen minutes.
- **Auditors** investigating a specific decision should locate the row by number or topic, follow the Implementation Anchor to the code, follow the Risk Mitigation Index to the surfacing surface, and follow the Reversibility cell to the reversal procedure. Total reading time: approximately three minutes per decision.

A reader who reads the entire log from top to bottom requires approximately thirty minutes; the log is not designed to be read sequentially in one sitting and the section headings support targeted navigation. The Closing Note at the end is the appropriate stopping point for any reader who wants a single-paragraph summary of what the log is for.

The nineteen rows below are the floor, not the ceiling. The first seventeen capture every non-trivial choice surfaced during the implementation of the twelve metric extractions, the inflection-detection step, the window-alignment step, and the four cross-cutting concerns (Observability, Onboarding, Visual Architecture, Executive Presentation). Rows 18 and 19 capture two design choices that were implicit in the template structure and were made explicit in response to QA Checkpoint 1 findings (the M6 placeholder aliasing rule and the pre-baked-rows design for the Requirements Traceability Matrix respectively). If a future re-run of the harness uncovers a new non-trivial decision, it is recorded as an additional row appended to the end of the table; mandatory rows are never removed or reordered.

### Style Conventions

The Decisions table and its supporting subsections follow a small set of style conventions that a reader should know before consulting the table. The conventions exist so that the log is uniform, scannable, and machine-checkable.

- **Code formatting** is applied to every SHA, file path, function name, environment variable, CLI flag, JSON field path, regex pattern, and Python constant. The backtick delimiters mark these as identifiers rather than ordinary prose; a search for any identifier in the log will find every reference deterministically.
- **Sentences within a cell** are separated by a period and a single space, with each sentence having an explicit subject and verb. Terse one-liners (for example, "Same as M2") are forbidden; cells that would otherwise be terse are expanded into complete sentences that name the antecedent.
- **Alternatives are enumerated** with `(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` markers within each Alternatives Considered cell. The chosen alternative is referenced in the Choice cell by the same marker so a reader can correlate the two cells.
- **Verbatim user instructions** that anchor a choice are placed inside double quotation marks and are attributed to the specific section of the Agent Action Plan (for example, "AAP §0.1.3") where the instruction appears. Paraphrased restatements are not used because paraphrase introduces interpretive risk.
- **Numeric thresholds** (for example, the 30-day reconciliation tolerance, the seven-day boundary-window threshold, the 20% unknown-rate ceiling for M6) are stated explicitly in the cell text rather than referenced symbolically; a reader does not have to look up the threshold elsewhere.
- **Tier labels** (High, Medium, Low) in the Reversibility column are followed by an em dash and a one-sentence justification. The justification names the specific reversal procedure rather than asserting reversibility abstractly.

These conventions are checked by the Decision Validation Procedures listed below the Decisions table. A future maintainer who edits the table should preserve the conventions; deviations from the conventions are themselves non-trivial decisions and would be recorded as their own row.

## Deviations from Literal Interpretation

The user's instructions are followed literally throughout the implementation except where two instructions conflict directly. In every such case, the resolution is documented as a decision-log entry above and is cross-referenced in the bulleted list below. The Explainability rule states that "any deviation from a literal or obvious interpretation of the requirements MUST have an explicit entry in the decision log" and that "unexplained deviations are treated as defects"; the three deviations enumerated below are the only deviations identified during implementation, and each is grounded in an explicit conflict between two user-stated requirements rather than in an interpretive judgment.

The pattern in every deviation is the same: a user-stated rule cannot be literally satisfied because another user-stated rule prohibits the literal satisfaction. In each case, the resolution honors the intent of the constrained rule (Onboarding intent, Observability intent, Visual Architecture intent) while respecting the binding rule (read-only constraint, batch-tool architecture, non-migration deliverable scope). The conflict, the intent, and the chosen resolution are documented so a reader can verify that no rule was silently abandoned.

- **Deviation 1 — Onboarding rule vs. read-only constraint.** The Onboarding rule directs implementers to "update existing onboarding docs (README, setup guides, wikis)" so that a new developer can go from a clean machine to a running application without asking questions. The user's separate boundary rule directs that the analyzed repository "MUST NOT" be modified. The repository's root `README.md` is an existing onboarding document; updating it would satisfy the Onboarding rule but violate the read-only constraint. **Resolution:** Co-locate new onboarding inside the report directory at `blitzy/reports/acceleration/README.md`. The co-located file is itself substantive (Purpose, Quickstart, Domain Context, Common Pitfalls, Architecture, Suggested Next Tasks) and is discovered immediately by any reader who navigates to the deliverable directory. See **Decision Row 14** above for the full rationale, alternatives evaluated, and reversibility analysis. The choice is Low-reversibility because the only path back is the user relaxing the read-only constraint.

- **Deviation 2 — Observability rule's prescriptive list vs. batch-tool architecture.** The Observability rule prescribes "structured logging with correlation IDs, distributed tracing across service boundaries, a metrics endpoint, health/readiness checks, and a dashboard template" as the minimum observability surface for every deliverable. Three of those five items (distributed tracing, metrics endpoint, health/readiness checks) presuppose a long-running service with HTTP surfaces and service-to-service calls; the harness is a batch CLI tool that runs to completion and exits, with no service surface to instrument. **Resolution:** Implement the two items that apply to a batch tool (structured JSON logging with `run_id` correlation IDs in `logs/<run_id>/` and a dashboard template in `dashboard.md`); document the non-applicability of the other three items with rationale rather than treating their absence as silent omission. See **Decision Row 13** above for the full rationale, alternatives evaluated, and reversibility analysis. The choice is Medium-reversibility because adding the three non-applicable items would require restructuring the harness as a long-running process.

### Methodological Notes

The Decisions table records each non-trivial choice in compact form. The notes below expand on three methodological points that span multiple rows and that a reader may want to understand before consulting the table.

The first methodological note concerns the **identical-methodology guarantee** that the user mandated for Baseline and After periods. Three of the nineteen rows (Row 2 window boundary handling, Row 12 engineering-actor selector, Row 16 UTC window boundaries) collectively realize this guarantee. The guarantee is not enforced by a runtime assertion; it is structural, in the sense that the extraction functions are parameterized over `(phase_name, date_range, actor_selector)` only and the selector itself contains exactly one branch on phase. Any future change that violated the guarantee would require modifying the same code path for both periods simultaneously, which is detectable by code review.

The second methodological note concerns **confidence assignment**. The user's instruction is that "a metric derived from direct counts in an issue tracker is High confidence. A metric approximated from git commit patterns is Medium confidence. A metric inferred from indirect proxies is Low confidence." This rule is implemented by the source-precedence chain in each per-metric extraction function (Rows 6, 8, 9 directly encode source-precedence fallbacks). The confidence tier is recorded in `data/metric_<N>.json#confidence` at extraction time and is the tier of the source actually used, not the tier of the source the metric "should" have. The dashboard's confidence-distribution pie chart and the report's per-metric caveat callouts read from this field.

The third methodological note concerns **the relationship between Metrics 3, 4, 5, and 7**. Metric 3 (Flow Predictability) is derived from Metric 2; Metric 5 (Flow Efficiency) is derived from Metrics 4 and 7; Metric 4 (Flow Active) and Metric 7 (Flow Time) are themselves independent extractions. The decision-log rows that affect M4 and M7 (Rows 12 engineering-actor selector and the not-explicitly-rowed history-rewrite-exclusion in M7) propagate to M5. The confidence tier of M3 is the same as M2's tier; the confidence tier of M5 is the minimum of M4's and M7's tiers. These propagation rules are implemented in `validate_consistency.py::check_derived_confidence()` and are not separately documented as decision rows because they are mechanical consequences of the user's instruction.

The fourth methodological note concerns **the boundary case where post-introduction history is shorter than 90 days**. The user instructed: "If fewer than 90 days of post-introduction data exist, report Baseline vs Post-Introduction only." `blitzy-cal` had 80 days of post-introduction history at the time of the AAP authoring (2026-02-25 inflection to 2026-05-15 cutoff); if the harness is re-run before 2026-05-25, the fallback applies. The harness computes `days_since_inflection = (cutoff_date - inflection_date).days` and selects the two-phase reporting mode when the value is less than 90. The selection is recorded in `data/windows.json#reporting_mode` (`three_phase` or `two_phase`); the report renderer reads this field and emits the appropriate section headings. This boundary handling is not a separate decision row because the user's instruction is unambiguous and the implementation is mechanical.

The fifth methodological note concerns **the treatment of merge commits in commit-volume calculations**. Per the user's instruction, per-module aggregation is weighted by "non-merge commits per module / total" (AAP §0.1.3). The harness uses `git log --no-merges --format='%H %s'` for commit-volume queries and `git log --merges` only for the merge-commit timestamps used by Metric 7. This consistent exclusion of merge commits from volume counts is applied across all per-module aggregations and is not a separate decision row because it follows directly from the user's instruction.

- **Agent Latitude.** The user's instruction that "the table above defines WHAT to measure, not HOW; you choose the extraction strategy for each metric based on available data sources." Decisions made under this latitude are tagged as such in the Decision Provenance section.
- **Baseline phase.** The temporal period before the AI tool introduction date (`9d80a5d026` dated 2026-02-25T00:24:31Z for `blitzy-cal`). All commits, PRs, reviews, and issues authored before this date are assigned to the Baseline phase.
- **Confidence tier.** One of High, Medium, or Low; assigned per metric based on the data source actually used at run time. The tier is recorded in `data/metric_<N>.json#confidence` and surfaces in the dashboard and the report's per-metric callouts.
- **Engineering actor.** The entity producing code on a PR. In the Baseline phase, the engineering actor is the human author; in the After phase, the engineering actor is Blitzy Agent for Blitzy-authored PRs and the human author for other PRs. The selector function `engineering_actor(pr, phase)` encodes this choice.
- **Flow Active.** The sum of the engineering actor's coding span durations across the working phases of a PR. Working phases are bounded by review events; gaps within a span are not subtracted.
- **Flow Efficiency.** The ratio `Flow Active / Flow Time` per PR; the median ratio across PRs in a phase is the metric value.
- **Flow Load.** The mean count of in-progress PRs (open OR draft with at least one commit) at the end of each Monday-aligned two-week window. Bots are excluded; Blitzy Agent is not a bot and is included.
- **Flow Predictability.** The reciprocal of the coefficient of variation (`mean / stdev`) of Flow Velocity across windows in a phase. Phases with fewer than four windows or zero variance report "Insufficient signal" rather than a numeric value.
- **Flow Time.** The wall-clock duration from the first commit on the PR branch to the merge commit on the default branch.
- **Flow Velocity.** The count of PRs merged to the default branch per Monday-aligned two-week window.
- **Inflection date.** The date at which the AI tool was introduced to the repository, derived per Row 1. The inflection date partitions the analysis window into Baseline and After phases.
- **Insufficient signal.** A metric status emitted when the data source required for a metric is unavailable, ambiguous, or below the threshold required for a meaningful computation. The status is paired with a `reason` field that names the source attempted.
- **Monday-aligned window.** A two-week interval starting on a Monday at 00:00:00 UTC and ending on the following Sunday at 23:59:59 UTC. Windows are generated by snapping the inflection date backward to the most recent Monday and emitting intervals both forward and backward.
- **Ramp-Up phase.** The first 90 days after the AI tool introduction date. Defined by the user as one of three temporal phases (Baseline, Ramp-Up, Steady State).
- **Reverts-of-reverts.** Revert commits whose original commit is itself a revert. These are excluded from Metric 8 per user instruction because they represent re-applications of previously reverted changes, not new defect indicators.
- **Steady State phase.** The period beyond 90 days after the AI tool introduction date. If fewer than 90 days of post-introduction data exist at run time, the harness reports Baseline vs. Post-Introduction instead of Baseline vs. Ramp-Up vs. Steady State.
- **Unattributable revert.** A revert commit whose original commit cannot be identified by either the body-line parse or the tree-hash lookup. Counted as a separate sub-count of Metric 8.

### Limitations of This Log

The decision log records every non-trivial implementation choice but does not capture three categories of information that a reader should be aware of when consulting it.

- **Trivial decisions are not recorded.** Choices that any competent engineer would have made the same way are not in the table; the Explainability rule's "competent engineer could reasonably have chosen differently" criterion defines the inclusion threshold. Examples of trivial choices that are deliberately omitted: the choice of Python 3 stdlib `logging` over a third-party logger; the choice to write JSON output rather than YAML; the choice to use ISO 8601 timestamps. If a reviewer believes any of these is non-trivial, the row can be added with rationale.
- **Counterfactual analyses are not exhaustive.** The Alternatives Considered cells enumerate the alternatives that were evaluated during implementation, not every conceivable alternative. A reviewer who wants to argue for an unconsidered alternative should add their argument as a new row in the Audit Trail rather than retroactively editing the Alternatives Considered cell.
- **Implementation-internal decisions are not in the log.** Choices about variable naming, function decomposition, code structure within a single function, and similar internal matters are recorded in the code itself (and in the docstrings) rather than in this log. The log is the source of truth for "why" decisions but not for "how the code is organized within a file"; the latter is governed by `scripts/_shared.py`'s structure and the per-script docstrings.

The three limitations above are intentional, not accidental. The decision log is a focused artifact: its purpose is to record the choices that distinguish this implementation from alternative implementations a different agent could have produced. The log is not a comprehensive design document.

### Closing Note

This decision log is the single source of truth for the "why" decisions behind the Development Acceleration Measurement. The Decisions table above is the primary surface; the Deviations, Reversibility Summary, Implementation Anchors, Risk Mitigation Index, Audit Trail, Future Watchpoints, Decision Provenance, Methodological Notes, Runtime Configuration Summary, Glossary, and Decision Validation Procedures subsections are supporting surfaces that elaborate specific aspects of the same set of choices. A reader who reads only the Decisions table can understand every non-trivial choice that was made; a reader who reads the full log can additionally locate the implementation of each choice, verify the mitigation of each risk, audit the review history, anticipate where new decisions may arise in future re-runs, trace each decision to its origin in user instructions or agent latitude, look up definitions for the technical terms used, and validate the log's internal consistency through the documented procedures.

The log will be updated as the harness is re-run against new windows of `blitzy-cal` history or against other repositories in the Blitzy-Sandbox organization. The nineteen mandatory rows are immutable in content and position (the first seventeen from the AAP, the next two appended in response to QA Checkpoint 1); additions are appended after Row 19 with their own provenance and reversibility detail. Any future amendment to an existing row is recorded as a separate "Decision Amendment" entry in the Audit Trail with the original row text and the amendment text preserved together so the history is fully auditable. The log's purpose is not to justify the implementation after the fact but to record the rationale that drove the implementation; the order of authorship (decision recorded before implementation written) is the structural enforcement of that purpose, and the Explainability rule's prohibition on embedding rationale in code comments is its structural prerequisite.
